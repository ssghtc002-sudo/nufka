What Internal Audit Actually Means
Internal audit is an independent, structured examination of how an organisation governs itself — its risk management, its control activities, its processes, and its operations.
It is not a smaller version of the statutory audit. An external audit exists to give an opinion on financial statements. Internal audit asks a broader question: are our processes doing what we believe they are doing, and are the risks that matter actually under control?
The answer goes to management and to those charged with governance, so they can act on it.

The COSO Internal Control Framework
Where a structured control assessment is required, we align our work with the COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework, the most widely applied reference model for evaluating control systems.
COSO breaks internal control into five components that operate together.
1. Control Environment
This is the foundation everything else sits on — the tone, structure, and expectations set from the top.
We look at:
Oversight and governance arrangements
How the organisation is structured
Where management responsibility sits
Ethical standards in practice
Accountability mechanisms
Delegated authority levels
Competence of people in key roles
2. Risk Assessment
Management is expected to identify and evaluate what could stop the organisation achieving its objectives.
Internal audit examines:
Financial exposure
Operational risk
Compliance obligations
Technology and cyber risk
Fraud scenarios
Strategic risk
Risks that are newly emerging
3. Control Activities
These are the specific policies and procedures that bring identified risks down to an acceptable level:
Approval routines
Authorisation limits
Reconciliations
Separation of incompatible duties
Access restrictions
Supervisory review
Physical safeguards
Exception reporting
4. Information and Communication
The right information has to reach the right people at the right time — otherwise controls fail silently.
We may assess:
Management reporting packs
Data accuracy and completeness
Escalation routes
Internal communication flows
Documentation standards
Clarity of reporting responsibility
5. Monitoring Activities
Controls degrade. Monitoring is how an organisation detects that before it matters:
Management review routines
KPI tracking
Exception reports
The internal audit function itself
Control self-assessment
Follow-up on corrective actions
Applying COSO gives management a disciplined method for locating control gaps — and for seeing how individual controls reinforce or undermine each other across the wider environment.

How the Ratings Are Applied
Low Risk — Minimal business consequence. Usually resolved through routine process improvement.
Moderate Risk — Capable of affecting efficiency, reporting quality, or compliance. Should be corrected within an agreed timeframe.
High Risk — A serious weakness with potential for financial loss, control breakdown, material error, fraud exposure, or regulatory consequence.
Critical Risk — Warrants immediate management attention given the possible financial, operational, legal, regulatory, or reputational fallout.

How We Run an Internal Audit
1. Understanding the business
We start with your structure, operating model, core processes, systems landscape, management objectives, and compliance obligations.
2. Risk assessment
Financial, operational, compliance, technology, and fraud risks are identified and weighted. This determines where audit attention is concentrated.
3. Audit planning
Scope and work programme are drafted against the risk profile and management\u0027s stated priorities.
4. Process walkthroughs
We sit with process owners and follow transactions through the system — approvals, documentation, configuration, exceptions, and all.
5. Control testing
Samples are selected and tested to confirm whether procedures are followed and controls operate as designed.
6. Root cause analysis
Reporting an exception is easy. We want to know why it happened.
Common underlying causes include:
Policies that were never written or never updated
System configuration that doesn\u0027t match the intended control
Segregation of duties that broke down as headcount changed
Absence of monitoring
Manual workarounds
Staff not trained on the process
Responsibility that was never clearly assigned
7. Risk-rated audit report
Findings are graded by significance and paired with recommendations that can realistically be executed.
8. Management action plan
Owners and target dates are assigned to each agreed corrective action.
9. Follow-up review
Where required, we return to verify that the agreed actions were implemented and are working.

Frequently Asked Questions About Internal Audit Services in Dubai
What are Internal Audit Services in Dubai?
Internal audit services provide an independent examination of how an organisation manages governance, risk, controls, and business processes. The work is designed to expose weaknesses, measure the risks attached to them, and recommend changes that strengthen both operations and the control environment.
What is the main purpose of an internal audit?
Its core purpose is to establish whether governance, risk management, and internal controls are genuinely working. Alongside that, internal audit highlights inefficient processes, compliance gaps, control breakdowns, and risks that are only beginning to emerge.
What is the COSO internal control framework?
COSO\u0027s Internal Control—Integrated Framework is the most widely used reference model for building and assessing internal controls. It is organised around five components: Control Environment, Risk Assessment, Control Activities, Information and Communication, and Monitoring Activities.
What is the difference between internal audit and external audit?
Internal audit examines governance, risk management, controls, and business processes, and delivers recommendations for improvement. External audit exists to give independent assurance on the financial statements under the applicable financial reporting and auditing requirements. The audiences and objectives differ, which is why the two are complementary.
How often should a company conduct an internal audit?
Frequency depends on size, complexity, industry, regulatory exposure, and risk profile. High-risk processes often warrant more frequent review, while lower-risk areas can be covered across a multi-year audit plan.
Can internal audit be outsourced?
Yes. The entire function can be outsourced, or you can adopt a co-sourcing model where external internal audit specialists work alongside your existing in-house team to add capacity or technical depth.
What does an internal audit report include?
A typical report sets out the observation, the risk it creates, the root cause, the recommendation, management\u0027s response, the responsible process owner, the target completion date, and a risk rating for each finding.
How does a risk assessment matrix help internal auditing?
The matrix scores each issue on likelihood and potential impact. That gives auditors and management a consistent basis for ranking findings and directing resources toward the matters that genuinely need attention first.
Which areas can NUFCA review during an internal audit?
Scope can extend across finance, procurement, sales, inventory, payroll, human resources, fixed assets, treasury, IT controls, compliance, governance, contracts, and any other process relevant to your risk profile.
